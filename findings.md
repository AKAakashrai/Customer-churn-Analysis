## 1. Overall Customer Churn Rate

### Actual Result

The dataset contains 7,043 customers, of which 1,869 customers have churned. This gives an overall customer churn rate of 26.54%.

### Business Interpretation

Approximately one in four customers has left the company. This indicates that customer churn is a significant business issue and provides a baseline for investigating which customer characteristics and behaviours are associated with higher churn.

## 2. Churn Rate by Contract Type

### Actual Result

Month-to-month customers had the highest churn rate at 42.71%,
followed by one-year contracts at 11.27%. Two-year contracts had
the lowest churn rate at just 2.83%.

### Business Interpretation

The analysis shows a strong association between contract length and
customer churn. Customers on month-to-month contracts are substantially
more likely to leave than customers on longer-term contracts. This may
be because month-to-month customers have greater flexibility to switch
providers without being committed to a long-term contract.

The company could consider strategies to encourage month-to-month
customers to move to longer-term contracts, such as offering discounts
or additional benefits for customers who commit to one- or two-year
contracts.

## 3. Churn Rate by Tenure Group

### Actual Result

Customers with shorter tenure had higher churn rates. The churn rate decreased consistently as customer tenure increased.

| Tenure Group | Churn Rate |
| ------------ | ---------: |
| 1–12 months  |        47% |
| 13–24 months |        28% |
| 25–36 months |        21% |
| 37–48 months |        19% |
| 49–60 months |        14% |
| 61–72 months |         6% |
| 0 months     |         0% |

### Business Interpretation

The analysis shows a strong association between customer tenure and churn. Customers who have been with the company for a shorter period are much more likely to churn than long-term customers.

Customers with 1–12 months of tenure had the highest churn rate at approximately 47%, while customers with 61–72 months of tenure had the lowest churn rate at approximately 6%.

This suggests that the early stages of the customer relationship are particularly important for retention. The company could focus on improving the onboarding experience and providing additional support or incentives during the first year to reduce early customer churn.

## 4. Churn Rate by Payment Method

### Actual Result

Customers using Electronic check had the highest churn rate at 45.29%. Customers using Mailed check had a churn rate of 19.11%, followed by Bank transfer (automatic) at 16.71% and Credit card (automatic) at 15.24%.

| Payment Method            | Churn Rate |
| ------------------------- | ---------: |
| Electronic check          |     45.29% |
| Mailed check              |     19.11% |
| Bank transfer (automatic) |     16.71% |
| Credit card (automatic)   |     15.24% |

### Business Interpretation

The analysis shows a strong association between payment method and customer churn. Customers using electronic checks have a substantially higher churn rate than customers using the other payment methods.

The company could investigate whether the electronic check payment process is associated with customer dissatisfaction or whether customers using this payment method have other characteristics that contribute to their higher churn rate.
